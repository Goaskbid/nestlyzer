Questions people ask

How does Nestlyzer decide where I should live?

You pick who is moving, for example a young family or a retired couple. Each profile weights categories such as costs, safety, family, jobs, mobility and environment. Nestlyzer scores every municipality in the country you choose and ranks them. Every weight and source is listed on the methodology page.

Which countries are covered?

63 countries (37 in Europe, plus Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, Colombia, Egypt, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Israel, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, Morocco, New Zealand, Philippines, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, United Arab Emirates, United States, Vietnam), with 145,533 places in all. Switzerland has the deepest data (28 official factors including municipal tax burden). Austria adds age structure. The other European countries use population, density and growth plus geographic measures such as airport and big-city distance; outside Europe the places, their population and local names come from GeoNames, with the same geographic measures.

Is it free and do I need an account?

It is free and there is no account. Your shortlist and custom profile stay in your browser.

Can I build my own profile?

Yes. Answer a few short questions about household, budget, daily anchor, mobility, tax sensitivity, climate, language and target countries, then fine-tune the weights. Nestlyzer shows which standard profiles are closest to yours.

Is this financial, tax or legal advice?

No. Nestlyzer is a decision aid built on public statistics. Check taxes, permits, schools, healthcare and housing with the responsible authority or a qualified adviser.

Where does the data come from?

35 public sources, each listed with its licence on the Method page: national statistics offices (among them the Swiss FSO and Tax Administration, Statistics Austria, Destatis, INSEE, ISTAT, INE, CBS, the US Census Bureau and Statistics Japan), Eurostat, the World Bank, the ECB, OpenStreetMap, Natural Earth, OurAirports and Wikidata. Photos and descriptions come from Wikimedia projects and never affect scores.
